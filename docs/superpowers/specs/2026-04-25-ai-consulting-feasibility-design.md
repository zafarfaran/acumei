# Feasibility Study: AI Consulting Company — "The AI Brain"

## Overview

Internal business plan and feasibility study for a solo-founder AI consulting company. The company's core proposition is **"building the AI brain of your company"** — not one-off AI tool integrations, but systemic intelligence embedded into a client's operations through agentic pipelines, workflows, and AI-powered processes.

The company operates as a contractor, partnering with other businesses to:
1. Identify operational bottlenecks that AI can solve
2. Design and build agentic pipelines and workflows
3. Drive AI adoption and transformation strategy
4. Provide ongoing support and optimization

### Founder Profile
- Solo founder with deep AI/ML engineering + software engineering background
- Can personally architect and build core AI systems
- Scales delivery through a bench of vetted contractors
- 6-12 months of financial runway (bootstrapped)

### Success Criteria (First 12 Months)
- Hit a meaningful revenue target (specific number to be modeled in Module 6)
- Land 3-5 paying clients
- Validate the "AI Brain" positioning in the market

### Geography
- Remote-first delivery model
- Flexible: local, national, or global depending on opportunity

---

## Deliverable Structure

The feasibility study is a **modular research package** — 7 independent but interconnected documents. Each module is self-contained, independently useful, and becomes a living reference post-launch. Modules can be prioritized based on highest uncertainty.

## Module 1: Market & Demand Analysis

**Purpose:** Answer "Is there a market, and who pays the most?"

### Contents

#### AI Adoption Landscape
- Current state of AI adoption across company sizes (SMB, mid-market, enterprise)
- Where companies are in their AI journey — what percentage still have zero AI integration
- Industry-by-industry adoption rates and maturity levels

#### Pain Point Mapping
- Specific operational bottlenecks driving companies to seek external AI help
- Categories: manual data processing, customer support volume, decision-making speed, repetitive knowledge work, data silos, slow internal processes
- Which pain points are most urgent and most underserved

#### Demand Signals
- Evidence that companies are spending on external AI consulting
- Job postings for "AI transformation" roles, consulting RFP trends, AI budget growth data
- Signals from platforms (Upwork, Toptal, etc.) showing demand for AI consulting

#### Segment Attractiveness Matrix
- Rank potential target segments (by industry and company size) across dimensions:
  - Willingness to pay
  - Ease of access (can a solo founder reach decision-makers?)
  - Typical project size
  - Repeat/expansion potential
  - Alignment with "AI Brain" positioning

#### "AI Brain" Resonance Test
- Which segments value systemic AI transformation vs. just wanting point solutions?
- The ideal client wants an embedded intelligence layer, not a chatbot
- Assess which industries have operations complex enough to benefit from the "brain" concept

#### Recommended Beachhead Segment
- Based on the analysis, identify 1-2 segments to target first
- Rationale for why these segments offer the best risk/reward for a solo founder launch

## Module 2: Competitive Landscape

**Purpose:** Answer "Who's out there, and where are the gaps I can exploit?"

### Contents

#### Competitor Categories
Map the playing field into tiers:

- **Big consultancies** (McKinsey, Deloitte, Accenture AI practices)
  - What they offer, typical engagement size ($500K+), where they're overkill
  - Strengths: brand trust, enterprise relationships
  - Weaknesses: slow, expensive, often deliver strategy without implementation

- **Boutique AI firms** (10-50 person shops)
  - Positioning, typical clients, pricing range ($50K-200K)
  - Strengths: specialized, hands-on
  - Weaknesses: still have overhead, may lack breadth

- **Freelancers/solopreneurs**
  - Platforms they use, typical rates ($75-200/hr)
  - Strengths: affordable, flexible
  - Weaknesses: limited scope, no systemic thinking, project-to-project

- **AI tool vendors** (self-serve AI platforms)
  - Indirect competition — the "build vs. buy" factor
  - When do companies choose tools over consulting?

#### Gap Analysis
- Hypothesis: there's a gap between expensive consultancies who deliver strategy decks and freelancers who do one-off implementations
- The "AI Brain" positioning lives in that gap — systemic, hands-on, affordable
- Validate or challenge this hypothesis with market evidence

#### Differentiation Framework
What makes the "AI Brain" offering distinct:
- Not a one-time project, but building persistent intelligence into the company
- Technical founder who builds, not a consultant who advises
- Lean cost structure (solo + contractors) enables mid-market pricing with enterprise-quality delivery
- End-to-end: from assessment through build through ongoing support

#### Competitive Risks
- AI tools becoming easier to self-serve (reduces need for consultants)
- Big firms moving downmarket with AI-specific practices
- Market flooding with AI freelancers as AI skills commoditize
- Open-source and no-code AI tools lowering the barrier

#### Positioning Statement
- Draft a clear one-liner capturing market position
- Should communicate: who you serve, what you do, why you're different

## Module 3: Service & Pricing Model

**Purpose:** Answer "What exactly do I sell, how do I price it, and what are my margins?"

### Contents

#### Service Ladder

A coherent product ladder with a free entry point:

0. **Free AI Opportunity Scan** (lead generation)
   - 30-60 minute call + brief written summary of top 3 AI opportunities
   - Enough to impress, not enough to self-implement
   - Designed to surface problems that need the paid Diagnostic
   - No cost to the client — pure lead-gen and trust-building

1. **AI Diagnostic** (the door-opener)
   - 2-4 week assessment of company operations
   - Identifies AI opportunities, delivers a prioritized roadmap
   - Pricing: project-based

2. **AI Brain Build** (the core offering)
   - Design and implement agentic pipelines, workflows, and AI systems
   - This is where the "brain" gets built — systemic, not piecemeal
   - Pricing: project-based or value-based

3. **AI Strategy & Adoption** (the wrapper)
   - Change management, team training, adoption roadmaps
   - Often bundled with the Build
   - Pricing: day-rate or project-based

4. **AI Brain Support** (the recurring revenue)
   - Ongoing monitoring, optimization, iteration on deployed systems
   - Pricing: monthly retainer

#### Pricing Research
- Market rates for comparable services across segments
- What SMBs vs. mid-market vs. enterprise will pay for each tier
- Benchmark against competitor pricing identified in Module 2

#### Pricing Model Per Service
- Specific recommended price ranges for each tier with rationale
- When to use hourly vs. project-based vs. value-based vs. retainer
- Discount/pilot pricing for early clients

#### Margin Analysis
For each service tier:
- Your time cost (hours * opportunity cost)
- Contractor costs (if applicable)
- Tooling/API costs (LLM APIs, cloud compute, etc.)
- Estimated gross margin per service

#### Revenue Scenarios — Client Archetypes
Model typical client journeys financially:
- e.g., Free Scan → Diagnostic ($5-15K) → Build ($20-80K) → Support ($3-8K/mo)
- Model 3-4 client archetypes (SMB, mid-market, enterprise, quick-hit)

#### Upsell/Expansion Logic
- How each service naturally leads to the next
- Building long-term client value and reducing acquisition cost
- The free scan as the top of funnel, retainer as the bottom

## Module 4: Operational Model

**Purpose:** Answer "How do I actually deliver all this as one person?"

### Contents

#### Delivery Model
Division of labor — solo founder + contractors:
- **Founder handles:** Client relationships, solution architecture, core AI design, quality control, sales, strategic decisions
- **Contractors handle:** Frontend/backend development, data engineering, domain-specific work, design, documentation
- **Trigger for contractors:** Project exceeds solo capacity, requires skills outside core competency, timeline demands parallel work

#### Contractor Strategy
- Where to find reliable AI/dev contractors (networks, platforms, referrals)
- Vetting process — how to assess quality before putting them on client work
- Management approach — how to maintain quality and consistency
- Build a reliable bench of 3-5 specialists you can call on repeatedly
- Contractor agreements — IP, NDA, payment terms

#### Capacity Planning
Realistic concurrent client modeling:
- **Scenario A:** 1 large client (full focus Build engagement)
- **Scenario B:** 2-3 medium clients (split across different phases)
- **Scenario C:** Mix of active Builds + retainer Support clients
- Hours per week breakdown for each scenario
- When to say no to new work

#### Tooling & Infrastructure
What's needed to run the business:
- Project management (Linear, Notion, etc.)
- Client communication (Slack Connect, email)
- AI development stack (APIs, frameworks, deployment)
- Billing and contracts (Stripe, legal templates)
- CRM (tracking pipeline and relationships)
- Cost estimate for the full stack

#### Quality Assurance
Maintaining delivery quality when scaling through contractors:
- Code review and architecture review processes
- Delivery standards and templates
- Client feedback loops
- Documentation requirements

#### Burnout Risk & Mitigation
- Honest assessment of the solo founder trap
- Warning signs: declining quality, missed deadlines, dreading client calls
- Mitigation: capacity limits, contractor scaling triggers, vacation planning
- Decision point: when to make a first full-time hire vs. stay contractor-based

#### Free Tier Capacity Management
- How many free AI Opportunity Scans per month without cannibalizing paid work
- Time budget per scan (prep + call + write-up)
- Expected conversion funnel: scans → diagnostics → builds
- When to throttle or pause free tier

## Module 5: Go-to-Market Playbook

**Purpose:** Answer "How do I actually find and land the first 3-5 paying clients?"

### Contents

#### Free Tier Funnel
The acquisition engine:
- **Free AI Opportunity Scan** — what's included (30-60 min call + brief written summary of top 3 AI opportunities)
- Enough to demonstrate expertise, not enough for DIY implementation
- Distribution channels: LinkedIn, communities, cold outreach, partnerships
- Conversion mechanics — how the free scan surfaces problems requiring paid Diagnostic
- Target conversion model: e.g., 10 free scans → 3 Diagnostics → 1-2 Builds

#### Channel Strategy
Ranked by effort vs. return for a solo founder:

1. **Content/thought leadership**
   - LinkedIn posts demonstrating AI knowledge and results
   - Blog/case studies (even from personal or open-source projects early on)
   - Short-form video showing AI in action (demos, before/after)

2. **Direct outreach**
   - Who to target (title, company profile)
   - Message templates — warm vs. cold
   - Volume expectations and response rates

3. **Partnerships**
   - Teaming with non-AI dev shops who encounter AI needs but can't deliver
   - Business consultants who advise on operations but can't build
   - SaaS companies whose clients need AI integration help

4. **Communities & events**
   - Which communities matter (industry-specific, AI-specific, founder-specific)
   - How to provide value without being salesy
   - Conference/meetup strategy

5. **Referrals**
   - How to engineer referrals from early clients
   - Referral incentives (if any)
   - Timing the referral ask

#### Sales Process
- Full journey: first contact → discovery call → free scan → proposal → close
- Typical sales cycle length per segment
- Proposal/SOW templates and approach
- Objection handling for common pushbacks

#### Early Wins Strategy
Landing the first clients with zero case studies:
- Discounted pilot engagements
- Leveraging personal network
- Open-source contributions and public demos as proof of capability
- Offering the free scan aggressively in the first 2 months

#### Brand & Positioning
- "AI Brain" messaging framework
- How to present the concept across channels (LinkedIn, website, proposals)
- Visual identity considerations (minimal — enough to look professional)

## Module 6: Financial Model

**Purpose:** Answer "Do the numbers work? When do I break even?"

### Contents

#### Cost Structure
Monthly fixed and variable costs:

**Fixed costs:**
- Tooling subscriptions (project management, CRM, design tools)
- AI API costs baseline (OpenAI, Anthropic, cloud compute)
- Domain, hosting, email
- Insurance (professional liability)
- Accounting/bookkeeping
- Legal (contracts, entity setup — amortized)

**Variable costs:**
- Contractor fees (per project, scaled to revenue)
- Marketing spend (paid ads, event attendance)
- Travel (client visits, if applicable)
- Additional API costs (scales with project volume)

**Personal draw:**
- Minimum monthly income needed to sustain yourself
- Target income at 12 months

#### Revenue Model
Build from the service ladder:
- Free scans → Diagnostics → Builds → Retainers
- Average deal size per service tier
- Expected close rate at each funnel stage
- Revenue per client over 12 months (customer lifetime value)
- Monthly recurring revenue trajectory from retainers

#### 3 Scenarios Over 12 Months

**Conservative:**
- Slow start (first paying client month 3-4)
- Lower conversion from free → paid
- Longer sales cycles, smaller deal sizes

**Base case:**
- First paying client month 2
- Reasonable conversion assumptions
- Mix of project and retainer revenue building over time

**Optimistic:**
- Strong network effects, fast referrals
- Larger deal sizes, shorter sales cycles
- Multiple concurrent clients by month 6

#### Break-Even Analysis
- Monthly revenue needed to cover costs + personal draw
- How many active clients does that require at each service tier?
- Expected month of break-even under each scenario

#### Cash Flow Timeline
- Month-by-month projection (12 months)
- When revenue starts vs. when expenses hit
- How runway depletes under each scenario
- When is the "danger zone" if revenue is slow?

#### Key Metrics to Track
The 5-6 numbers that tell you if the business is working:
1. **Pipeline value** — total potential revenue in active conversations
2. **Conversion rate** — free scan → paid, at each stage
3. **Average deal size** — are you trending up or down?
4. **Monthly recurring revenue** — retainer base growing?
5. **Utilization rate** — what % of your time is billable?
6. **Gross margin** — revenue minus direct delivery costs

## Module 7: Risk Assessment & Go/No-Go Framework

**Purpose:** Answer "What could kill this, and should I actually do it?"

### Contents

#### Risk Matrix
Each risk scored on likelihood (low/medium/high) and impact (low/medium/high):

**Market risks:**
- AI hype cycle cools, companies pull back budgets
- Self-serve AI tools make consulting unnecessary for common use cases
- Economic downturn reduces discretionary spending on AI transformation

**Competitive risks:**
- Big consultancies move downmarket with AI-specific offerings
- Market floods with AI freelancers, race to the bottom on pricing
- A well-funded startup productizes the "AI Brain" concept

**Delivery risks:**
- Overcommit and underdeliver as a solo founder
- Contractor quality issues damage client relationships
- Scope creep on projects erodes margins

**Financial risks:**
- Sales cycle longer than expected, runway burns before profitability
- Clients delay payments, cash flow crunch
- Underpricing early deals sets wrong market expectations

**Solo founder risks:**
- Burnout from wearing every hat
- Key-person dependency — illness or personal issue halts the business
- Revenue ceiling from capacity constraints

**Technical risks:**
- AI landscape shifts rapidly, tools/frameworks you build on get deprecated
- Client data security incident
- AI output quality issues in production

#### Mitigation Strategies
For each high-likelihood or high-impact risk:
- Specific, actionable mitigation plan
- Early warning indicators
- Contingency actions

#### Go/No-Go Decision Framework

**Green lights — launch:**
- Validated demand from N free scans converting to paid interest
- Identified a clear beachhead segment with willingness to pay
- First paid LOI or signed contract
- Differentiation is clear and defensible

**Yellow lights — proceed with caution:**
- Demand exists but sales cycle is longer than modeled
- Market is crowded but differentiation holds up
- Revenue projections work only in base/optimistic scenarios
- Need to narrow service offering to be viable

**Red lights — don't launch yet or pivot:**
- Free scans don't convert to paid interest
- Can't articulate differentiation that resonates with prospects
- Costs exceed what the market will bear
- Every segment says "we'll do it in-house"

#### Decision Timeline
- At what point in the runway should you make the go/no-go call?
- What milestones trigger the decision? (e.g., "If I don't have a paid client by month 4, re-evaluate")
- Built-in review checkpoints (monthly? quarterly?)

#### Pivot Options
If the original model doesn't validate:
- Productize one service into a SaaS tool
- Focus on training/education instead of building
- Niche down to a single industry vertical
- Join an existing AI firm and build from the inside
- Open-source an "AI Brain" framework and monetize support/enterprise features

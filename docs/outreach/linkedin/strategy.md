# Posting & Niche Strategy

Which audience to chase first, in what order to post the 30 cards, and what to do around the posts (the posts alone won't land clients — the DMs around them will).

---

## 1. The niche call: lead with IT companies, wedge in with training

You have three offers and two very different audiences:

| Track | Posts | Buyer | Deal size | Sales friction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SMB automation | 1–20 | Salon / trades / restaurant owners | £2–8k builds | High — trust-heavy, slow |
| IT workflow builds | 21–26 | CTOs, eng managers, agency/MSP owners | £5–15k pilots | Medium |
| AI training seminars | 27–30 | Same IT buyers | £1.5–2.5k per day | **Low** |

**Target IT companies first, and open with the training offer.** Four reasons:

1. **Your network is already this audience.** You come from big tech. Your LinkedIn connections are engineers, eng managers, and people who became CTOs at small companies. For the SMB audience you'd be building distribution from zero; for the IT audience you already have it. This is the single biggest factor.
2. **The buyer lives on LinkedIn.** CTOs and agency owners scroll LinkedIn daily and buy from it. Plumbers and salon owners mostly don't — they're on Facebook and Instagram, and they buy from referrals and Google. LinkedIn posts aimed at SMBs build credibility, but rarely DMs.
3. **Training is the cheapest possible "yes".** One day, fixed price, zero integration risk, no access to production systems needed. A cautious CTO can approve it without a procurement dance. Compare that to asking a stranger for £8k and access to their CRM.
4. **Training upsells itself.** Every seminar room contains your next workflow-pilot client. You spend a day with their team, you see their broken processes first-hand, and you leave behind a playbook with your name on it. "We should automate that ticket triage we talked about" is a warm conversation two weeks later.

**The chain:** training day (£2k, easy yes) → workflow pilot (£5–15k, post-26 offer) → retainer / more workflows. Each step is bought by someone who already paid you once.

### Where the SMB track fits

Don't drop it — but be honest about the channel. LinkedIn SMB posts are for **credibility and shareability** (your network knows salon owners even if salon owners don't follow you), not direct lead gen. The SMB pipeline runs through the **outbound platform** you built (`outbound/`): cold email sequences, the funnel in `funnel-and-templates.md`. LinkedIn is the air war for IT; email is the ground war for SMB. Run both, expect clients from different doors.

Within SMB, when you do push: **trades first** (electricians, plumbers, heating engineers). The missed-call pain is the most quantifiable (£20k+/yr story, posts 4 and 13), the fix is the simplest build, and trade businesses refer each other constantly.

## 2. The posting calendar (8 weeks, 3 posts/week)

Tue and Thu mornings 8–10am UK get the prime IT/training content (B2B decision-makers check LinkedIn before standup). Saturday carries the SMB/brand posts (weekend reach is smaller but cheaper, and owners do scroll on Saturday mornings).

Sequencing rules baked in below: personal intro first (always overperforms), explainers before offers, asks only after 2–3 weeks of value, never two asks in the same week.

| Week | Tue (IT/training) | Thu (IT/training) | Sat (SMB/brand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1** — personal intro | **22** — MCP explainer | **3** — 49% stat |
| 2 | **21** — IT track intro | **28** — the training gap | **17** — pull quote |
| 3 | **23** — hidden sprint tax | **12** — two-minute test | **5** — the biggest myth |
| 4 | **24** — ticket, end to end | **27** — seminar announcement | **13** — invisible ledger |
| 5 | **29** — the curriculum | **25** — seats vs systems | **2** — three quick wins |
| 6 | **30** — training ask | **15** — can / can't | **16** — a week, handled |
| 7 | **26** — pilot offer ask | **14** — anatomy of an automation | **4** — missed call maths |
| 8 | **19** — five questions | **18** — £37/hour | **10** — discovery call ask |

Remaining SMB posts (6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 20) refill Saturdays in weeks 9–11, with post 20 last — it's written as the arc-closer.

Why this shape:
- Post 1 leads everything. Personal "I started something" posts are the highest-reach post type on LinkedIn; it warms the whole calendar.
- The MCP explainer (22) runs before the IT intro (21). Lead with usefulness, not an offer — the person who saves post 22 reads post 21 differently.
- The training ask (30) lands in week 6 after announcement → argument → curriculum. The pilot ask (26) follows a week later, catching anyone for whom training felt too small.
- Two asks never share a week, and every ask week still has a value post beside it.

## 3. What to do around the posts (this is where clients come from)

Posting without outreach is a billboard in a desert. The posts are conversation starters; these are the conversations:

**The 50-DM list (do this before week 1).** List 50 people you actually know — ex-colleagues, old managers, uni friends — who now work at or run 10–100-person software companies, agencies, or MSPs. After post 21 goes live, DM them personally: *"You might've seen I've gone solo building AI workflow stuff. If your team's drowning in triage/reporting I'd love 20 minutes to show you what I'm building — and honestly, any feedback helps."* Warm DMs convert 10–100× better than any post. Your first training booking almost certainly comes from this list, not from the feed.

**15 minutes of commenting daily.** Comment usefully on posts from CTOs, eng managers, and the #buildinpublic / agency crowd before and after you post. LinkedIn reach follows engagement reciprocity, and your comment under a CTO's post is an ad your target buyer actually reads.

**Connection requests: 10–15/day**, targeted: CTO / Head of Engineering / founder at UK software agencies and MSPs, 10–100 headcount. No pitch in the request — let the content do the warming. Accept rate is much higher with a note like "enjoyed your post on X" when true.

**Engagement → DM rule.** Anyone who likes or comments on posts 21–30 and fits the buyer profile gets a DM within 24h: *"Thanks for the comment on the MCP post — out of curiosity, is your team using agents for anything real yet?"* Question, not pitch. The pitch comes on the call.

**Booking link in first comment, always.** Never in the post body (LinkedIn suppresses external links).

**Reply to every comment within the first hour.** First-hour engagement decides reach. Schedule posts for mornings you can babysit them.

## 4. Scorecard and pivot rules

Check at the end of week 4 and week 8:

| Signal | Healthy by week 4 | If not |
|---|---|---|
| Warm DM conversations started | 15+ | Expand the 50-DM list; you're under-DMing, not under-posting |
| Inbound profile views from target titles | Rising week over week | Tighten headline: "AI workflows + training for software teams" |
| Discovery/intro calls booked | 2–4 | Push harder on warm network; add 5 DMs/day |
| First training day sold | By week 6–8 | Offer the first one at a "founding client" rate to a warm contact — the case study is worth more than the margin |

**Pivot rules:**

- If by week 8 the IT track has produced calls but no sale → the offer is fine, the price or packaging isn't. Try a 90-minute paid "AI readiness session" (£300–500) as a smaller wedge before the full day.
- If the training sells but pilots don't follow → fine. Stack training days; 4/month is a real income while pilot trust builds.
- If SMB email outreach (the outbound platform) starts converting faster than LinkedIn → follow the money. The strategy serves the pipeline, not the other way round.

**One rule above all:** the first three paying clients come from people who already know you. The content's job is to make you easy to say yes to when you ask. So ask.

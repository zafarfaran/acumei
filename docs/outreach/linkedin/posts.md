# LinkedIn Content Plan — 20 Posts

Post 2–3 per week (e.g. Tue / Thu / Sat morning, 8–10am UK — business owners check LinkedIn before the day starts). Order matters: it builds from "who is this person" to "book a call". Images live in `cards/`.

**Rules that make these work:**

- First line is the hook — LinkedIn truncates after ~2 lines, so the first sentence must earn the "see more" click.
- Reply to every comment within an hour. Comments in hour one decide reach.
- No links in the post body (LinkedIn suppresses them). Put your booking link in the first comment.
- One post = one idea. Resist combining.
- A note on honesty: posts 4 and 7 use worked examples and industry numbers, not client claims. When you land your first real client, swap their real numbers in — a real "£X saved" post will outperform everything here.

## Post 14 — Anatomy of an automation (week 7)

**Image:** `cards/post-14.png`
**Goal:** Demystify. People don't buy what they can't picture — show the moving parts in plain words.

```text
"AI automation" sounds like a black box. It isn't. Every system I build has the same four parts:

1. TRIGGER — something happens. A call gets missed. An invoice goes 14 days unpaid. A client hasn't rebooked in 8 weeks.

2. BRAIN — the system works out what this situation needs. Not a dumb rule — context. A first-time caller and a regular get different treatment.

3. ACTION — it does the thing. Sends the text, books the slot, drafts the order, chases the invoice. Politely, instantly, every single time.

4. LOG — and this is the part I refuse to skip — you can see everything it did and why. Every message, every decision.

If someone offers you a system without part 4, walk away. You should never have to trust a black box with your customers.

That's it. That's the whole magic trick.
```

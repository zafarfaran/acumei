# Module 7: Risk Assessment & Go/No-Go Framework

## Executive Summary

- **The three highest-severity risks are: (1) extended sales cycle depleting runway before first Build revenue, (2) solo founder burnout and key-person dependency creating a single point of failure, and (3) self-serve AI tools eroding the need for implementation consulting.** Each has a concrete mitigation plan, and none is fatal if addressed proactively.
- **Recommendation: CONDITIONAL GO** — the business is viable under all three financial scenarios (conservative through optimistic) with $40K runway, the market gap is validated, and unit economics are strong. However, launch should be contingent on completing 5 specific pre-launch validation steps within 60 days.
- **The critical decision point is Month 5.** If no Build engagement has been signed by Month 5 (the single variable that most determines Year 1 outcome), the founder must execute a pre-defined pivot or cost-reduction plan before runway enters the danger zone.

## Detailed Mitigation Strategies

For each Critical and High severity risk, the following provides early warning indicators, active mitigation steps, and a contingency plan if the risk materializes.

### Critical: D1 — Overcommit and Underdeliver

**Why it's critical:** As a solo founder, there is no buffer. Taking on one project too many degrades quality on all projects simultaneously, damaging the reputation that is the business's primary asset.

**Early warning signals:**
- Weekly billable hours exceed 35 for 2 consecutive weeks
- Client deliverables slip by more than 3 business days
- Founder skips non-billable activities (free scans, content creation) for 2+ weeks
- More than 2 active Build/Diagnostic projects running simultaneously

**Active mitigation:**
1. Maintain a rolling 4-week capacity forecast updated every Monday
2. Never verbally commit to a start date without checking the capacity forecast
3. Build 20% buffer time into every project estimate (if the Build takes 6 weeks of work, schedule 7.5 weeks)
4. For each new opportunity, explicitly answer: "What am I *not* doing if I take this?"

**Contingency (if it happens):**
1. Immediately contact affected clients and reset expectations with a revised timeline — transparency preserves trust
2. Bring in an emergency contractor within 48 hours for delegable execution tasks
3. Defer all new pipeline development for 2 weeks to clear the backlog
4. Post-mortem: identify which commitment was the tipping point and adjust the capacity cap

### Critical: S1 — Burnout

**Why it's critical:** The founder *is* the business. Burnout doesn't just reduce productivity — it eliminates revenue entirely. Recovery from burnout typically takes 3-6 months, which exceeds runway.

**Early warning signals:**
- Working more than 50 hours in any single week
- Dreading client calls (emotional signal)
- Deliverable quality noticeably declining (re-work rate increasing)
- Skipping exercise, sleep, or personal commitments for 3+ consecutive weeks
- Revenue is up but satisfaction is down

**Active mitigation:**
1. Hardcode 1 full day off per week and 1 week off per quarter into the calendar *before* booking clients
2. Outsource non-core tasks immediately: bookkeeping, scheduling, basic admin
3. Build AI-powered internal tools for the repetitive parts of the job (proposal generation, report templates, code scaffolding)
4. Maintain a peer network of 3-5 other solo consultants for mutual support and accountability
5. Monthly "energy audit": score each activity (1-10) and eliminate or delegate anything scoring below 4

**Contingency (if burnout begins):**
1. Reduce client load to 1 active project immediately
2. Activate contractor bench for all delegable work on remaining projects
3. Take 1 full week off (retainer clients get self-service dashboards; active projects get a 1-week pause with client notification)
4. If recovery requires more than 2 weeks: inform retainer clients, pause new sales, and consider a 1-month sabbatical funded from cash reserves

### High: M2 — Self-Serve AI Tools Make Consulting Unnecessary

**Why it's high:** This is the structural existential risk. If ChatGPT, Copilot, or a platform like Zapier Central makes it trivially easy for mid-market companies to build their own AI workflows, the consulting model loses its foundation.

**Early warning signals:**
- Prospects saying "we're going to try it ourselves first" at increasing rates (>30% of free scans)
- No-code AI platforms raising large rounds and showing mid-market traction
- Free scan conversion rate dropping below 15% (Module 6 warning threshold)
- Average deal size declining below $10K for Diagnostics

**Active mitigation:**
1. Position on complexity, not capability — "You can build a chatbot yourself; we build the system that connects 7 departments and 4 data sources"
2. Move up the value chain: strategy, architecture, and change management are harder to automate than implementation
3. Incorporate self-serve tools *into* client deliverables — become the expert who configures and connects them, not competes with them
4. Track the AI tool landscape monthly and proactively incorporate new tools into the methodology

**Contingency:**
1. Pivot to "AI architecture advisory" — shorter, higher-margin engagements focused on design, not build
2. Develop training products (see Pivot Option #2) that help companies use self-serve tools effectively
3. Niche deeper into a vertical where domain expertise creates a moat self-serve tools cannot match

### High: C2 — Market Floods with AI Freelancers

**Why it's high:** Barriers to entry are low. Every developer with ChatGPT experience is now an "AI consultant." This compresses pricing and makes it harder to differentiate.

**Early warning signals:**
- Upwork/Toptal AI consultant listings grow >50% year-over-year
- Prospects comparing pricing to freelancer rates ($75-150/hr)
- Win rate on proposals drops below 25%
- Average time to close extends beyond 8 weeks as prospects evaluate more options

**Active mitigation:**
1. Never compete on price — compete on outcomes. Every proposal includes projected ROI, not just hourly rates
2. The service ladder (Free Scan to Diagnostic to Build to Support) is itself a differentiation — freelancers do not offer structured progression
3. Build a portfolio of case studies showing systemic results (not just "I built a chatbot")
4. Focus on referral-based acquisition (Module 5) where the recommendation short-circuits comparison shopping
5. Develop proprietary methodologies and frameworks (the "AI Brain" assessment, the "AI Readiness Scorecard") that cannot be replicated by a generic freelancer

**Contingency:**
1. Niche to a single vertical (see Pivot Option #3) where domain expertise creates an insurmountable advantage
2. Increase retainer percentage of revenue — retainer clients are less likely to switch to a freelancer mid-relationship
3. Partner with 2-3 high-quality freelancers as subcontractors rather than competing with them

### High: D2 — Contractor Quality Issues

**Why it's high:** Client trust is with the founder. If a contractor delivers poor work under the founder's brand, the damage is asymmetric — it takes months to build trust and one bad deliverable to destroy it.

**Early warning signals:**
- Contractor deliverables requiring >30% rework in code review
- Client feedback mentioning quality inconsistencies
- Contractor missing deadlines by more than 2 business days
- Contractor communication gaps (unresponsive for >24 hours during active projects)

**Active mitigation:**
1. Pre-engagement vetting: paid trial project ($500-1,000) before any client work assignment
2. Every contractor deliverable goes through founder review before client delivery — this is non-negotiable
3. Fixed-price SOWs with milestone payments (not hourly) — aligns incentives and caps exposure
4. Maintain a bench of 3-5 vetted contractors across key skill areas (frontend, backend, data engineering)
5. Weekly check-ins with contractors during active projects; daily standups during critical phases

**Contingency:**
1. Remove underperforming contractor immediately — do not attempt to coach during an active client project
2. Absorb the remaining work personally (this is why the 2-project cap matters)
3. Proactively communicate with the client: "We're bringing in our senior architect (the founder) to ensure quality on this deliverable"
4. Post-mortem: update vetting criteria and trial project requirements

### High: D3 — Scope Creep Erodes Margins

**Why it's high:** Scope creep is the silent margin killer in consulting. At $70K Build projects with 50-65% target margin (Module 3), a 25% scope overrun turns a $35K profit into a $17K profit — cutting income nearly in half.

**Early warning signals:**
- Hours tracked exceed estimate by >10% at the project midpoint
- Client requesting "small additions" more than twice per week
- SOW "Excluded" items starting to appear in client emails as expectations
- Founder spending more than 20% of project hours on unplanned tasks

**Active mitigation:**
1. SOW includes explicit "Included/Excluded" lists and a numbered change order process
2. Change orders priced at 1.2x standard rate (premium for disruption) with written client approval before work begins
3. Weekly scope check: compare actual work against SOW line items; flag any deviation immediately
4. Build 15-20% scope buffer into every estimate (quote 7 weeks for 6 weeks of work)
5. At the 50% project milestone, conduct a formal scope review with the client and re-confirm remaining deliverables

**Contingency:**
1. If scope has already crept: have an honest conversation with the client, document the additions, and present a change order for the overage
2. For the current project, absorb reasonable overages to preserve the relationship; apply learnings to the next SOW
3. If a client pattern emerges (chronic scope creep), adjust the client's next engagement to value-based pricing with defined outcomes, not defined tasks

### High: F1 — Sales Cycle Longer Than Expected

**Why it's high:** The financial model assumes first Diagnostic revenue by Month 2-3 (base/conservative). If the first deal closes at Month 5 instead of Month 3, cash on hand drops from $29,200 to approximately $18,000 under conservative assumptions with $40K runway — still viable but uncomfortably thin.

**Early warning signals:**
- 0 Diagnostics closed by end of Month 3
- Free scan conversion rate below 15% after 10+ scans completed
- Prospects requesting "more time to think" or "need internal approval" at increasing rates
- Pipeline weighted value below $30K at Month 3

**Active mitigation:**
1. Begin free scan outreach in the pre-launch period (Month -1 to Month 0) to prime the pipeline
2. Run 8-10 free scans/month instead of 6-8 to compensate for lower conversion rates
3. Pursue at least 2 warm network leads in parallel with inbound from Day 1
4. Offer founding client pricing (25% discount) with urgency: "3 founding client slots available, 1 already filled"
5. If no Diagnostic by Month 3: activate a "fire sale" — offer a $5,000 mini-Diagnostic (2-day intensive) to get a client on the books and generate a case study

**Contingency:**
1. At Month 4 with no revenue: cut personal draw to $3,500/mo (below minimum, but buys 2 additional months)
2. Pursue a fractional/part-time consulting role (2 days/week) to generate $6-8K/mo while continuing to build the pipeline
3. If Month 5 with no revenue: trigger the hard go/no-go decision (see Decision Timeline)

## Go/No-Go Decision Framework

### Green Lights — Launch

All of the following must be true before committing full-time to the business. These can be validated during a 60-day pre-launch validation period while still employed or during the first 30 days.

| # | Criterion | Measurement | Threshold |
|---|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| G1 | Free scan demand validated | Completed free AI Opportunity Scans | 5+ scans completed with 2+ (40%+) expressing interest in paid Diagnostic |
| G2 | Beachhead willingness-to-pay confirmed | Direct pricing conversations during scans | At least 2 prospects have seen pricing ($10-20K Diagnostic range) without rejecting it; 1+ has said "send me a proposal" |
| G3 | At least 1 signed engagement or LOI | Signed SOW, LOI, or paid deposit | 1+ signed document or payment received (even a $5K mini-Diagnostic counts) |
| G4 | Unit economics confirmed | Margin analysis on first 1-2 engagements (or detailed estimates) | Gross margin above 55% on Diagnostics (solo-delivered) and above 45% on projected Builds (with contractor costs) |
| G5 | Positioning resonates | Qualitative feedback from 10+ conversations | 7+ out of 10 prospects can articulate what makes the "AI Brain" offering different when asked; 0 prospects confuse the offering with generic "AI chatbot" services |
| G6 | Financial runway confirmed | Bank balance | $30,000+ liquid cash available at launch date (per Module 6 minimum recommendation) |
| G7 | Legal/operational foundation set | LLC, insurance, contracts | LLC formed, E&O insurance active, SOW template reviewed by attorney |

**Decision: If all 7 green lights are met, proceed with full launch.**

### Yellow Lights — Proceed with Caution

Any of the following trigger a "conditional launch" with modified parameters:

| # | Condition | Implication | Modified Approach |
|---|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Y1 | Only 3-4 free scans completed (not 5+) and 1 shows paid interest | Pipeline is thinner than ideal | Launch, but maintain a part-time income source (fractional consulting, contract work) for the first 3 months while building pipeline. Increase free scan volume to 10+/month. |
| Y2 | No signed engagement yet, but 2+ proposals are outstanding | Sales cycle is slower than modeled | Launch, but use the conservative financial scenario as the operating plan. Cut personal draw to $4,000/mo for Months 1-3. Set a hard deadline: if no signed deal by Month 3, trigger Yellow-to-Red reassessment. |
| Y3 | Runway is $20-30K (below the $30K recommended minimum) | Cash buffer is thin under conservative scenario (lowest point: $9,200 with $20K) | Launch only with at least 1 signed engagement generating revenue in Month 1-2. Without a signed deal, do not launch — the conservative scenario with $20K runway has a dangerously low $9,200 cash floor (Module 6). |
| Y4 | Pricing conversations suggest the market expects lower rates (Diagnostic at $5-8K vs. $10-20K) | Market may be more price-sensitive than modeled | Launch, but adjust to SMB pricing tier initially ($5-10K Diagnostics, $25-50K Builds) and validate whether mid-market pricing works with different positioning or a different beachhead segment. |
| Y5 | Only 1 beachhead segment shows traction (e.g., legal firms respond but accounting firms don't) | Narrower addressable market than assumed | Launch focused exclusively on the responding segment. This is not necessarily bad — niche focus can accelerate reputation building. Revisit broader market after 3-5 clients in the niche. |

**Decision: If 1-2 yellow lights are present with no red lights, launch is acceptable with the specified modifications. If 3+ yellow lights are present, treat as a red light.**

### Red Lights — Pause or Pivot

Any single red light means "do not launch full-time" without first resolving the issue:

| # | Condition | Implication | Required Action |
|---|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| R1 | Fewer than 3 free scans completed in 60 days of active outreach | Demand signal is absent or outreach is fundamentally broken | Do not launch. Diagnose: Is the target segment wrong? Is the offer unclear? Is the distribution channel broken? Conduct 20+ additional conversations before re-evaluating. |
| R2 | 0 out of 5+ prospects express willingness to pay for a Diagnostic | The service is not solving a problem prospects will pay to fix | Do not launch. Pivot the offer: consider training/workshops instead of consulting, or target a different segment. The "AI Brain" concept may need repositioning. |
| R3 | Every pricing conversation results in rejection at $10K+ Diagnostic level | Pricing is fundamentally misaligned with market | Do not launch at current pricing. Test at $5K for a "mini-Diagnostic" (1-week sprint). If $5K also rejected, the problem is demand, not price — return to R2. |
| R4 | Runway below $20K with no signed engagement | Risk of running out of cash before first revenue under any scenario | Do not launch full-time. Pursue hybrid model: maintain part-time income while running 3-5 free scans per month as a side activity. Re-evaluate when either (a) a signed deal materializes or (b) runway reaches $30K+. |
| R5 | Founder has unresolved personal commitments that prevent 40+ hrs/week for 6 months | The business requires full-time intensity in Year 1 | Do not launch until commitments are resolved. A half-effort launch is worse than no launch — it burns runway without generating sufficient pipeline. |
| R6 | After 30 days of conversations, the competitive landscape has a direct competitor offering the same service at lower price in the same beachhead segment | Timing or positioning is wrong | Do not launch into that segment. Either (a) identify a different beachhead where the competitor is absent, or (b) develop a clear differentiation that prospects articulate back to you unprompted. |

- All green: Plan for Year 2 growth. Set hiring timeline. Consider office space.
- Any yellow: Year 2 is viable but growth will be slower than planned. Maintain solo model. Focus on retainer acquisition.
- Any red: Year 2 as a full-time solo business is not viable at current trajectory. Begin transitioning to the highest-feasibility pivot option within 60 days.

## Pivot Options

Ranked by feasibility for a solo technical founder with deep AI/ML + SWE skills, assuming the consulting model has not achieved target traction by Month 8-10.

### Pivot Option #1: Niche to Single Vertical (Feasibility: 9/10)

**What it means:** Abandon the broad "mid-market professional services" beachhead and focus exclusively on one sub-vertical (e.g., only law firms, only accounting firms, or only mid-market financial services).

**Why highest feasibility:**
- Requires zero new skills or infrastructure — same services, narrower target
- The consulting model is *already working* at some level; the problem is likely too-broad positioning, not wrong model
- Vertical specialization accelerates referrals (law firms talk to other law firms)
- Enables "best in class" positioning: "the AI consultant for law firms" vs. "an AI consultant"
- Can be executed in 2 weeks (update positioning, website, outreach targets)

**When to execute:** If Month 5 checkpoint shows traction with one segment but not others, or if conversion rates are low due to positioning confusion.

**Risk:** The chosen vertical may be too small or too resistant to AI adoption.

### Pivot Option #2: Focus on Training/Education (Feasibility: 7/10)

**What it means:** Shift from implementation consulting to AI training workshops, courses, and enablement programs for mid-market companies.

**Why high feasibility:**
- Leverages the same expertise and relationships built during free scans and Diagnostics
- Shorter sales cycle: training purchases are lower-commitment ($2-10K per workshop vs. $70K Build)
- Scalable: one workshop can serve 10-20 participants; online courses can serve unlimited
- Can be launched within 30 days using existing content from Diagnostics and free scan materials
- Compatible with a hybrid model (part-time training revenue + part-time consulting pipeline)

**Revenue model:** $3-5K for half-day workshops, $5-10K for full-day intensives, $500-2K for online courses. Target: 3-4 workshops/month = $12-20K/month revenue at 80%+ margin.

**When to execute:** If free scans generate interest in "learning about AI" but not in "hiring someone to build AI," training is the product the market is asking for.

**Risk:** Training market is competitive and commoditizing rapidly. Margins are lower per hour than consulting.

### Pivot Option #3: Productize into SaaS (Feasibility: 5/10)

**What it means:** Take the "AI Brain" framework — the assessment methodology, the pipeline templates, the integration patterns — and build it into a self-serve software product that mid-market companies can use without a consultant.

**Why moderate feasibility:**
- The founder has deep SWE skills to build the product
- Consulting engagements provide direct user research for product requirements
- Recurring SaaS revenue ($500-5K/mo per customer) is more scalable than consulting
- Could be launched as an MVP within 3-4 months of focused development

**Why not higher:**
- Requires significant upfront development time with no revenue during build phase
- SaaS sales cycle for mid-market is 3-6 months (Module 5 sales cycle data)
- Competes with well-funded AI platform startups (Relevance AI, etc.)
- Consulting and SaaS are very different businesses — attempting both splits focus
- Requires marketing, support, and product development skills beyond consulting

**When to execute:** If multiple clients ask "can we just use your framework ourselves?" — that is the market signal for productization.

**Risk:** High burn rate during development phase. Product-market fit is not guaranteed. Requires different go-to-market.

### Pivot Option #4: Join Existing AI Firm (Feasibility: 8/10)

**What it means:** Abandon the solo model and join an existing AI consultancy or boutique firm as a senior consultant, principal, or partner — bringing the methodology, client relationships, and pipeline built during the solo attempt.

**Why high feasibility:**
- Immediate income stability — eliminates runway risk
- The consulting experience, case studies, and methodology developed during the solo attempt are highly valuable to a firm
- Mid-market AI boutiques are actively hiring experienced consultants (Module 2: boutique firms growing into this space)
- Can be positioned as a strategic choice rather than a failure: "I validated the market, now I'm joining a platform to scale"
- Can be executed within 30-60 days

**When to execute:** If Month 8 checkpoint shows red lights and the founder assesses that the problem is scale/sales capacity rather than market demand. Also appropriate if the founder discovers they prefer delivery over business development.

**Risk:** Loss of independence and equity upside. Compensation is likely $150-250K rather than the $275-716K Year 1 revenue range of the solo model.

### Pivot Option #5: Open-Source Framework + Monetize (Feasibility: 4/10)

**What it means:** Open-source the "AI Brain" assessment framework, pipeline templates, and integration components. Monetize through paid support, hosted cloud versions, enterprise features, and consulting on top of the framework.

**Why lower feasibility:**
- Open-source community building takes 6-12+ months to reach meaningful adoption
- Monetization of open-source is notoriously difficult (the "1% rule" — only 1% of users pay)
- Requires significant investment in documentation, community management, and DevRel
- Does not generate revenue in the short term — exacerbates the runway problem
- The founder's advantage is business context + AI expertise, not a novel technology

**When to execute:** Only if there is genuine technical innovation worth open-sourcing (e.g., a novel AI pipeline framework that solves a problem existing tools do not). This is a long-term play, not a pivot from a failing consulting business.

**Risk:** Dilutes the competitive moat (anyone can use the framework). Community building is a full-time job. Monetization timeline is 12-24+ months.

## Overall Recommendation

### Verdict: CONDITIONAL GO

The business should launch, subject to completing the 7 green-light criteria within a 60-day pre-launch validation period.

### Reasoning

**The case for GO:**

1. **Market demand is validated.** The $6.4B AI consulting market growing at 24-27% CAGR (Module 1) with a confirmed "missing middle" gap at $15-100K (Module 2) provides a clear market opportunity. The 50% of mid-market companies stuck in "pilot purgatory" need exactly what this business offers.

2. **Unit economics are strong.** Gross margins of 70-85% on solo-delivered services and 50-65% on contractor-augmented Builds (Module 3) are exceptional. A single mid-market client generates $73-147K in 12-month LTV. The business needs only 3-5 active clients to be highly profitable.

3. **The financial model works under all scenarios.** Conservative: $275K Year 1 revenue, break-even Month 5. Base: $397K, break-even Month 3. Optimistic: $716K, break-even Month 1 (Module 6). Even the conservative case generates $143K in net cash by Month 12.

4. **Operational overhead is minimal.** At $1,000/mo in growth-phase fixed costs (Module 4), the business has a low burn rate that extends runway significantly. Monthly break-even (including $5K personal draw) is only $5,400 in the early months.

5. **The service ladder creates a natural flywheel.** Free Scan to Diagnostic to Build to Retainer (Module 3) generates compounding MRR that de-risks the business over time. By Month 12, the base case has $18K MRR from 3 retainers — retainer revenue alone exceeds break-even.

**The case for CONDITIONAL (not unconditional) GO:**

1. **Two risks are rated Critical.** Solo founder overcommitment (D1) and burnout (S1) are inherent structural risks that cannot be fully mitigated — only managed. The business has zero redundancy.

2. **Pre-launch validation is essential.** The financial model assumes first Diagnostic by Month 2-3, but this is only possible if pipeline-building starts before Day 1. Without pre-launch validation (5+ free scans, 1+ signed engagement), the model's assumptions are untested.

3. **The $40K runway is adequate but not generous.** Under the conservative scenario, the lowest cash point is $29,200 (Month 2) — a comfortable margin. But with $20K runway, the lowest point drops to $9,200, which is dangerously close to zero after accounting for unexpected expenses.

4. **The competitive landscape is intensifying.** AI freelancers are flooding the market (C2, rated High severity) and self-serve tools are improving rapidly (M2, rated High severity). The window for establishing a consulting practice with defensible positioning is 12-24 months, not indefinite.

### What Must Be True for Success

The following 5 conditions are necessary (not merely helpful) for Year 1 viability:

1. **First Build engagement signed by Month 5.** Build revenue is 55-59% of Year 1 revenue across all scenarios. Without at least one Build in the first 5 months, the retainer flywheel never starts, and the business remains dependent on one-time Diagnostic revenue that does not compound.

2. **Free scan pipeline produces 6+ scans/month by Month 3.** At 25-35% conversion (Module 5), 6 scans/month yields 1.5-2 Diagnostics/month — the minimum to keep the funnel moving. Below this volume, there is not enough pipeline to convert.

3. **Blended gross margins stay above 50%.** If margins fall below 50% due to scope creep, underpricing, or contractor cost overruns, the business cannot sustain the founder's personal draw while covering costs. This is the financial kill line.

4. **The founder maintains 60-75% utilization without exceeding 45 hours/week.** Below 60% means not enough revenue. Above 75% means no time for pipeline development. Above 45 hours/week means burnout is approaching. All three boundaries must hold simultaneously.

5. **At least 1 retainer client active by Month 8.** The retainer flywheel ($6K/mo per retainer) is what transforms the business from "feast or famine" project revenue to a sustainable operation. Without retainers, Year 2 requires the same level of constant new-client acquisition as Year 1 — which is not sustainable for a solo founder.

### Single Biggest Risk and How to Manage It

**The single biggest risk is the interaction between "time-to-first-Build" (F1) and "solo founder burnout" (S1).**

Here is why these interact dangerously: if the first Build takes longer to close than expected (F1), the founder compensates by increasing outreach intensity and accepting suboptimal engagements. This increases hours worked and cognitive load. The pressure of declining runway adds stress. By the time the first Build arrives, the founder may be operating at 80%+ utilization with depleted energy — exactly when they need to deliver their best work on the highest-value engagement. Poor Build delivery damages the client relationship, which kills the retainer conversion, which eliminates the flywheel that makes the business sustainable.

**How to manage it:**

1. **Pre-launch pipeline development.** Conduct 5-8 free scans before Day 1 so the sales cycle clock starts before the runway clock.
2. **Financial buffer.** Start with $40K+ runway (not $30K) to provide 7+ months of minimum burn without revenue, removing the pressure to close deals prematurely.
3. **Emotional circuit breakers.** If weekly hours exceed 50 or stress levels are high for 2 consecutive weeks, cancel all non-essential meetings and take 2 days off. This is counterintuitive when runway is burning, but a burned-out founder closes zero deals.
4. **Month 5 hard checkpoint.** If no Build is signed by Month 5, execute a pre-defined plan (hybrid income, cost reduction, or pivot) rather than grinding harder. The plan should be written and committed to *before* launch — deciding in a moment of stress leads to poor decisions.
5. **Monthly founder health check.** Every month, answer honestly: "Am I excited to do this work today?" If the answer is "no" for 3 consecutive months, that is a signal to explore Pivot Option #4 (join an existing firm) regardless of financial metrics.

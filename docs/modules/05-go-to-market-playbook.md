# Module 5: Go-to-Market Playbook

## Executive Summary

- **Primary acquisition channel is local networking (Chamber of Commerce, BNI, trade associations) combined with free discovery calls** — this combination generates the highest-volume, lowest-friction leads for a solo founder targeting small/local businesses in Phase 1 (Months 1-6), with local networking alone capable of producing 8-12 discovery calls per month within 60 days of consistent attendance.
- **The funnel math requires 8-12 discovery calls per month to yield 3-5 SMB projects per month** — at a 30-40% discovery-to-project conversion rate, 10 calls/month produces 3-4 projects in Month 1 and 5-7 projects/month by Month 4-6.
- **Expect 1-3 days from discovery call to signed project** — the sales cycle for local SMB businesses is dramatically shorter than mid-market: a free 30-45 minute call, a same-day proposal, and a decision within days. No procurement, no committee, no weeks of follow-up.
- **Mid-market channels (LinkedIn content, direct outreach) activate in Phase 2 (Month 6+)** but LinkedIn content should begin from Day 1 to build audience momentum for the upmarket transition.

## Phase 1: SMB Go-to-Market (Months 1-6)

### The SMB Sales Mechanism: Free Discovery Calls

The free 30-45 minute discovery call is the primary lead-generation and sales mechanism for SMB clients. It replaces the more elaborate Free AI Opportunity Scan used for mid-market prospects — local business owners do not need a written brief; they need a conversation that shows you understand their business and can fix their pain.

**What the discovery call includes:**
- 5 minutes: Introduction, rapport building, learn about their business
- 15-20 minutes: Walk through their daily operations — where do they spend the most time on repetitive tasks? What falls through the cracks? What do they wish they could automate?
- 10-15 minutes: Identify 2-3 specific automations you could build, explain what they would do in plain language, and give a rough price range
- 5 minutes: Next steps — "I can have a one-page proposal to you by end of day"

**What makes it convert:**
The call works because (a) it is free and low-commitment, (b) the founder demonstrates understanding of their specific business (not generic AI talk), (c) the founder speaks in outcomes ("this saves you 10 hours/week") not technology ("we'll use a RAG pipeline"), and (d) the same-day proposal creates momentum before the business owner gets busy and forgets.

**Cost to deliver:** 45-60 minutes of founder time per call ($75-$100 in opportunity cost at $100-$150/hr SMB-adjusted internal rate). This is manageable at 8-12 calls/month (8-12 hours/month on lead generation).

### Channel Strategy: SMB Priority Order

Channels are ranked by expected ROI for a solo founder targeting local/small businesses with no existing local business network and 6-12 months of runway.

#### 1. Local Networking (PRIMARY Channel)

**Why this is #1:** Local networking is the single highest-ROI channel for SMB AI consulting because (a) local business owners buy from people they know and trust, (b) repeat attendance builds compound visibility, (c) one good relationship generates multiple referrals, and (d) competition from other AI consultants at local business events is near zero.

**Where to network:**

| Venue | Cost | Frequency | Expected Lead Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chamber of Commerce events** | $200-$500/year membership | 2-4 events/month | 2-4 conversations per event; 1-2 discovery calls/month |
| **BNI (Business Network International)** | $500-$700/year + weekly breakfast | Weekly meeting | 1-3 referrals/month once established (Months 2+) |
| **Trade association meetups** (local plumber/HVAC/contractor groups, restaurant owner groups) | $0-$200/year | Monthly | 1-3 conversations per event; highly targeted |
| **Rotary Club / Lions Club** | $200-$400/year | Weekly or biweekly | Slower build; high-trust relationships; 1-2 referrals/quarter |
| **Small business workshops / SBA events** | $0-$50/event | Monthly | 3-5 conversations per event; mix of quality |
| **Local coworking spaces** | $0 (free events) to $200/mo (membership) | Ongoing | Ambient networking; 1-2 leads/month from casual conversations |

**Networking rules for AI consultants targeting SMB:**
1. **Never lead with "AI."** Lead with the problem you solve: "I help small businesses automate the stuff that eats up their time — scheduling, follow-ups, quoting, that kind of thing." The word AI intimidates many small business owners.
2. **Ask questions, don't pitch.** "What's the most tedious part of running your business?" is worth more than any elevator pitch.
3. **Offer the discovery call immediately.** "I'd love to spend 30 minutes learning about your business — no charge, no obligation. Worst case, you get some ideas. Can I buy you a coffee next Tuesday?"
4. **Follow up within 24 hours.** Send a LinkedIn connection, an email, or a text. Most people who were interested at the event will forget within 48 hours if you do not follow up.
5. **Be a regular.** The first time you attend, you are a stranger. The third time, you are a familiar face. The sixth time, you are "the AI guy/gal." Consistency matters more than charm.
6. **Give referrals to get referrals.** Refer business to others in your networking group. This builds social capital and triggers reciprocity.

**Expected ramp:**
- Month 1: 3-5 discovery calls from networking (plus personal contacts)
- Month 2: 5-8 discovery calls (word of mouth starts)
- Month 3+: 8-12 discovery calls (BNI referrals flowing, chamber connections established)

#### 2. Word of Mouth / Referrals

**Why this is #2:** In local business communities, word of mouth is the dominant buying signal. A plumber who hears from his accountant that "there's a guy who built us an automated quoting system for $2,500" will call you. This channel starts slow but compounds faster than any other.

**How to engineer word of mouth:**

| Tactic | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Ask every completed client for 2 referrals** | Within 1 week of project delivery | "Who else do you know who deals with [the problem we just solved]?" — specific is better than generic |
| **Provide a referral script** | At project completion | Give the client a 1-sentence description they can text to a friend: "My buddy [Name] built me an AI system that does [outcome]. He's doing a free 30-min call for business owners. Want his number?" |
| **Incentivize with service credit** | For retainer clients | "$100 off your next month's retainer for every referral that becomes a project" |
| **Make the work visible** | During and after delivery | With permission, post a brief case summary to your local business group: "Just helped [Business Type] automate their [process]. Saved them ~10 hours/week." |
| **Cross-pollinate at networking events** | Ongoing | Mention (with permission) specific results: "I just finished a project for a local HVAC company — their quoting process went from 2 hours to 10 minutes." Others in the room self-identify as having similar problems. |

**Expected output:** 0-1 referrals/month in Month 1-2, growing to 2-4 referrals/month by Month 4-6 as the portfolio grows.

#### 3. Local Digital Presence

**Why this is #3:** Many small business owners spend time in Facebook groups, Nextdoor, and Google searches. A visible local digital presence captures demand that networking misses — business owners who are searching for help but do not attend networking events.

**Channels and tactics:**

| Channel | Tactic | Time Investment | Expected Results |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Local Facebook groups** (e.g., "[City] Small Business Owners") | Join 3-5 groups. Post helpful content (not ads): "3 things any small business can automate today without writing code." Answer questions. Offer free discovery calls in comments when relevant. | 2-3 hrs/week | 1-3 discovery calls/month by Month 2 |
| **Nextdoor (business section)** | Create business profile. Post helpful local content. Respond to business-related questions. | 1 hr/week | 0-1 leads/month (slow but zero competition) |
| **Google Business Profile** | Create profile as "AI Automation Consultant." Collect reviews from every client. Optimize for "[city] business automation" searches. | 1 hr/month (maintenance) | 0-2 inbound inquiries/month by Month 4+ (builds over time with reviews) |
| **Yelp / local directories** | Basic listing presence. | 30 min (one-time) | Minimal direct leads but supports credibility |

**Content approach for local digital:**
- Write in plain language. No jargon. No "leveraging AI-powered solutions."
- Focus on outcomes and specific businesses: "How a local plumber saved 10 hours/week with automated scheduling"
- Include your location in every post. You want to be found by "[city] AI consultant" searches.
- Always end with a soft CTA: "Happy to chat if any of this sounds relevant to your business."

#### 4. Free Workshops / Lunch-and-Learns

**Why this is #4:** A 30-60 minute workshop positions you as the local expert, delivers value to a room full of potential clients simultaneously, and generates 3-8 discovery calls per event. The ROI per hour is high once you have the material built.

**Workshop format:**

**Title:** "How AI Can Save Your Business 10 Hours a Week" (or similar outcome-focused title)

**Structure (45-60 minutes):**
- 10 min: "What AI actually is (and isn't) for small businesses" — demystify, de-risk
- 15 min: 3 real examples of local businesses using AI (scheduling, follow-ups, quoting, review management) — use your own case studies when available, industry examples when not
- 10 min: Live demo — show a real automation in action (e.g., an AI that drafts personalized follow-up emails from a simple CRM entry)
- 10 min: "Is AI right for your business? 5 questions to ask yourself" — self-assessment framework
- 5 min: Q&A + offer free discovery calls to anyone interested

**Where to host:**
- Local library meeting rooms (free)
- Chamber of commerce as a member benefit (free or nominal fee)
- Coworking spaces (often free for community events)
- Local restaurants during slow hours (partner with the owner — they provide the space, you bring foot traffic)
- BNI chapter as a guest speaker slot

**Cadence:** 1 workshop per month starting Month 2. Once the material is built, each workshop costs 2-3 hours of prep + delivery time and generates 3-8 discovery calls.

**Expected conversion:** Of 15-25 attendees, 20-30% will request a discovery call. Of those, 30-40% will become paid projects.

#### 5. Partnerships with Local Professional Services

**Why this is #5:** Accountants, bookkeepers, local business consultants, and IT providers already have trusted relationships with small business owners. A single partnership can generate 1-3 referrals per month with zero outreach effort.

**Partner types for SMB:**

| Partner Type | What They Bring | What You Bring | Example Referral Scenario |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accountants / Bookkeepers** | Trusted relationship with every local business owner; know their pain points and budget | AI automation that saves their clients time/money (and makes the accountant's life easier) | Accountant's client complains about chasing invoices → accountant refers you to build automated invoicing + follow-up |
| **Local IT providers / MSPs** | Existing service contracts with local businesses; technical trust | AI capability they cannot deliver | IT provider's client asks "can you add AI to our scheduling?" → they refer to you |
| **Business consultants / SCORE mentors** | Advisory relationships with new/growing businesses | Technical implementation capability | Consultant recommends automation to a client but cannot build it → refers to you |
| **Insurance agents** | Know every local business owner; trusted advisor | Complementary service (no competition) | Agent hears client complain about admin burden → mentions your free discovery call |
| **Web developers / marketing agencies** | Build websites/marketing for local businesses; see their operations | AI automation that extends their client relationships | Web dev finishes a website project → "by the way, you should talk to [you] about automating your follow-ups" |

**How to approach:**

> "Hi [Name], I'm [Name] — I help local businesses automate their most time-consuming tasks using AI. Things like scheduling, follow-ups, quoting, invoicing. I know you work with a lot of local business owners, and I imagine some of them are drowning in manual work. Would you be open to grabbing a coffee? I'd love to learn about your clients and see if there's a way we can help each other's businesses. No pitch — just exploring the fit."

**Partnership development timeline:**
- Month 1: Identify 8-10 local accountants, bookkeepers, IT providers, and business consultants. Reach out to all.
- Month 2: Coffee meetings with 4-6. Explain what you do in plain language. Ask about their clients' pain points.
- Month 3: Formalize 1-2 partnerships with simple referral agreements (10-15% of first project or reciprocal referrals).
- Month 4-6: First referrals flow. Nurture partnerships with regular check-ins and success stories.

#### 6. Content / LinkedIn (Secondary — Building for Phase 2)

**Why this is #6 for Phase 1:** LinkedIn and content marketing are essential for the Phase 2 mid-market push, but they are secondary for SMB lead generation because (a) most local business owners are not on LinkedIn, (b) content takes 3-6 months to build momentum, and (c) the founder's time is better spent on local networking in Phase 1.

**However, start LinkedIn content from Day 1** for three reasons:
1. By Month 6, when mid-market outreach begins, you will have 6 months of content history and a growing audience
2. SMB case studies can be repackaged as LinkedIn content that demonstrates mid-market delivery capability
3. It costs only 3-5 hours/week and compounds over time

**Phase 1 LinkedIn approach (repositioned for local business audience):**

**Profile optimization:**
- **Headline:** "I help local businesses automate the work that eats up their time | AI automation for plumbers, HVAC, salons, restaurants & trades"
- **About section:** Lead with the pain point ("You didn't start your business to spend 3 hours a day on scheduling and follow-ups"), present the solution ("I build custom AI automation that handles the tedious stuff"), close with CTA for the free discovery call.
- **Featured section:** Pin the free discovery call offer, one case study (once available), and one high-performing post.

**Posting cadence:** 3 posts per week. Mix of:
- SMB case studies and before/after results (1x/week when available)
- "How AI can help [specific business type]" educational posts (1x/week)
- Behind-the-scenes build logs showing real automation in action (1x/week)

**By Month 6, transition LinkedIn positioning toward mid-market** — the SMB case studies become the proof that you can deliver.

#### 7. Direct Outreach to Mid-Market (Phase 2 — Month 6+)

**Why this is last for Phase 1:** Selling $15-50K+ engagements to mid-market firms with zero case studies is extremely difficult. Direct outreach to mid-market is deferred to Month 6+ when the founder has 15-20+ completed SMB projects and a proven delivery track record.

See "Phase 2: Moving Upmarket" section below for the full mid-market GTM strategy.

### SMB Objection Handling

#### Objection 1: "I can't afford it"

**Why they say it:** Genuine budget constraint, or sticker shock because they have never bought a technology service before.

**Response:**

> "I totally get that — $2,500 is a real investment for a small business. Let me ask you this: you mentioned you spend about 10 hours a week on [the manual process]. What's your time worth per hour? [Usually $50-150 for a business owner.] So that's $500-$1,500 a week you're spending on work a computer could do. The automation pays for itself in 2-4 weeks, and then it just keeps saving you money.
>
> If cash flow is tight right now, I can split it into two payments — half to start, half when it's done. And if it doesn't save you at least 5 hours a week, I'll work on it until it does."

**Key principle:** Reframe as ROI and payback period. Offer payment flexibility. Never discount without changing scope.

#### Objection 2: "My business is too small for AI"

**Why they say it:** They associate AI with big tech companies. They think AI is for enterprises, not plumbers.

**Response:**

> "That's actually the opposite of how it works. The big companies already have teams of people handling this stuff — they can afford to be inefficient. It's small businesses like yours where AI makes the biggest difference, because every hour you save goes straight to your bottom line.
>
> I just helped a [similar business type] automate their [similar process]. They're a [X]-person shop, just like you. It took me [X] days to build and now it saves them [X] hours a week. You don't need to be a tech company to use AI — you just need someone who knows how to build it for your specific business."

#### Objection 3: "I don't trust AI"

**Why they say it:** They have seen scary headlines, bad chatbot experiences, or they fundamentally distrust technology they do not understand.

**Response:**

> "I hear you, and honestly, a lot of the AI stuff in the news is hype. What I build is not Terminator stuff — it's practical automation. Think of it like a really smart assistant that handles your [scheduling / follow-ups / quoting / whatever their pain is].
>
> Here's what I'd suggest: let me show you a quick demo of what it would look like for your business. Takes 5 minutes. If it feels weird or wrong, no pressure at all. But most business owners, once they see it handling something that used to take them 2 hours in about 30 seconds, they get pretty excited."

**Key principle:** Demystify. Use analogies they understand. Offer a low-commitment demo.

#### Objection 4: "What if it breaks?"

**Why they say it:** Legitimate concern. Their business depends on things working. They have been burned by technology before.

**Response:**

> "Great question — reliability is the most important thing. Here's how I handle it: First, I build it with fallbacks. If the AI part ever has an issue, the system alerts you and falls back to the manual process — so your business never stops. Second, I test everything thoroughly before it goes live, and we do a trial run together. Third, I offer a support retainer [$200-500/mo] that includes monitoring, fixes, and updates. If something breaks, I fix it — usually within hours, not days.
>
> And honestly, the manual process you're using right now 'breaks' too — it's just that you're the one fixing it every time, at 3 hours a day. The automation is actually more reliable than doing it by hand."

### Proposal Template for SMB

**Structure (1 page, never longer):**

1. **What You Told Me** (2-3 sentences): Restate their problem in their words. "You're currently spending ~10 hours/week manually scheduling appointments and sending follow-up texts. Some leads fall through the cracks because you're too busy on a job to respond."

2. **What I'll Build** (bullet list, 3-5 items): Plain language. "An automated scheduling system that lets customers book online, sends confirmation texts, and follows up automatically if they don't show." No jargon.

3. **Timeline:** "I'll have this built and working within [X] days."

4. **Investment:** "$X,XXX — includes everything: build, testing, training, and 30 days of free support after launch."

5. **Payment Terms:** "50% ($X,XXX) to start. Remaining 50% when you're happy with the result."

6. **Next Steps:** "To get started, just sign below and send the deposit via [Zelle / Venmo / invoice]. I'll start building within 48 hours."

## Phase 2: Moving Upmarket (Month 6+)

### Transition Strategy

By Month 6, the founder should have 15-20+ completed SMB projects, 5-8 documented case studies, 5-10 active retainers, and a proven delivery track record. This portfolio is the foundation for the mid-market push.

**The key positioning shift:** You are no longer a consultant with no track record asking for $50K. You are a consultant with 20+ completed AI projects, documented results, and references — who is now offering the same capability to larger organizations at a larger scale.

### Mid-Market Free Tier Funnel

The Free AI Opportunity Scan is the primary lead-generation mechanism for mid-market prospects. It converts cold or warm prospects into qualified pipeline by delivering genuine value before asking for money.

**Deliverables (as defined in Module 3):**
- Pre-call: 15-minute review of company website, product, and public information
- Live call: 45-60 minute structured conversation mapping current operations, pain points, and data assets
- Post-call: 1-2 page "AI Opportunity Brief" delivered within 48 hours — top 3 AI opportunities with rough impact sizing

**What makes it convert:**
The brief is designed to be *compelling but not actionable*. It surfaces specific, quantified opportunities (e.g., "Your document review process could be 70% faster with AI-assisted extraction, saving approximately $400K/year in associate time") but does not provide the architecture, data requirements, or implementation plan. The prospect sees the prize but needs the Diagnostic to reach it.

**The SMB portfolio advantage:** When presenting the brief, the founder can reference specific, relevant case studies: "I built a similar automation for a local [business type] and it reduced their [process] time by 70%. For your firm, the same approach at scale could save $X/year." This transforms the conversation from theoretical to proven.

**Cost to deliver:** 2-3 hours of founder time per scan ($400-600 in opportunity cost at $200/hr internal rate).

### Mid-Market Distribution Channels (Month 6+)

| Channel | Tactic | Expected Volume | Ramp Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn content** (already running for 6 months) | Shift positioning toward mid-market audience. Publish SMB case studies reframed for mid-market relevance. Direct CTA to free AI Opportunity Scan. | 3-5 scan requests/month by Month 8-9 | Already warmed up |
| **Direct outreach** | Personalized messages to mid-market prospects referencing specific case studies | 2-4 scans/month from Month 7 | 4-6 weeks to first results |
| **Referrals from SMB clients** | SMB clients who know people at larger firms | 1-2 scans/month from Month 7+ | Organic; dependent on SMB relationships |
| **Partnerships (non-AI dev shops, management consultants)** | Partner firms refer mid-market clients for free scans | 1-2 scans/month from Month 8+ | 2-4 months to develop |
| **Events/Communities** | Offer free scans to attendees after industry talks | 0-2 scans/month (intermittent) | Requires speaking opportunities |

**Total target:** 4-8 free mid-market scans per month by Month 8-9, scaling to 8-12 per month by Month 12.

**Target:** 2-4 clients who have paid for at least an AI Diagnostic ($10-25K) within Months 6-12.

### Mid-Market Objection Handling

#### Objection 1: "We can do this in-house"

**Response framework:**

> "That's great that you have internal capability — it actually makes the engagement more effective because your team can maintain and extend whatever we build. Here's what I typically see: internal teams are excellent at running your core operations, but AI implementation is a specialized discipline that's evolving weekly. The average company that tries to build AI in-house takes 3-4x longer and spends 2-3x more than working with a specialist, because of the learning curve.
>
> What I'd suggest is this: let's start with the Diagnostic. If your team can execute the roadmap themselves, you'll have a concrete plan. If they need support on the complex initiatives, I can build those or work alongside your team to upskill them."

#### Objection 2: "It's too expensive"

**Response framework:**

> "I understand — this is a significant investment. The Free Scan identified approximately [$X] in annual savings. The Diagnostic investment of [$Y] is roughly [1-2%] of that annual opportunity.
>
> If budget is a constraint, we can start with a narrower-scope Diagnostic focused on your single highest-impact opportunity, which brings the investment to [$lower number]. Or we can structure payment across two months.
>
> What I would not recommend is waiting. Every month those manual processes continue costs approximately [$monthly cost]."

#### Objection 3: "We've tried AI before and it didn't work"

**Response framework:**

> "That's more common than you might think. Most failed AI projects fall into one of three categories: (1) poor use case selection, (2) adoption failure, or (3) point solutions that weren't integrated into workflows.
>
> My approach is different: the Diagnostic phase exists precisely to avoid these failures. And I can show you 20+ projects I've delivered in the last 6 months — real production systems, not demos. The pattern of what works and what doesn't is clear from that experience.
>
> Given your past experience, the Diagnostic is designed to be valuable on its own — a clear-eyed assessment informed by someone who's delivered dozens of AI projects."

#### Objection 4: "How do I know you can deliver as a one-person shop?"

**Response framework:**

> "Fair question. Let me answer it with numbers: I've delivered 20+ AI automation projects in the last 6 months, every one on time and on budget. I have [X] active support retainer clients. Here are [2-3 specific case studies with results].
>
> The one-person model is an advantage: you get the senior person doing the work, not a partner who sells and a junior team that delivers. I also work with vetted specialist contractors for larger Builds.
>
> And the Diagnostic is a contained 3-4 week engagement. You'll see my work quality firsthand before committing to a larger Build."

#### Objection 5: "We're not ready for AI yet"

**Response framework:**

> "I hear that a lot. Based on our Free Scan conversation, your [specific process] involves [specific manual work] that accounts for approximately [X hours/week]. That's not a readiness question — that's an opportunity question.
>
> 'Ready' for AI in 2026 means: you have data (even in spreadsheets and email), processes that follow patterns, and people whose time is better spent elsewhere. You checked all three boxes.
>
> That's exactly what the Diagnostic assesses — a realistic readiness picture and a phased approach that meets you where you are."

## Brand & Positioning

### "AI Brain" Messaging Framework

The "AI Brain" metaphor is the core positioning concept. It differentiates from competitors who sell "AI tools," "chatbots," "automation," or "AI strategy." The brain metaphor communicates:

1. **Systemic, not point-solution:** A brain is interconnected — it is not a single tool but a network of capabilities
2. **Intelligent, not just automated:** A brain learns, adapts, and makes decisions
3. **Central, not peripheral:** A brain is at the core of operations — this is foundational infrastructure
4. **Persistent, not project-based:** A brain grows over time — this is an ongoing relationship
